What a story point costs the fleet

The intake refiner started pointing tickets on 2026-10-06 (#1273). To see whether the rubric means anything, every completed ticket with run data was pointed after the fact from its written spec, without looking at what it cost, and then compared with what the Smiths actually spent: dollars, working time and tokens.

How this was done

297 tickets: every issue in codefrak-oss/frakworks closed as completed, labelled chore, bug or feature, with Smith runs in the engine (188 chores, 49 bugs, 60 features). Proposals, research and intake issues are left out. Ten agents pointed 30 tickets each from the ticket's Goal, Acceptance criteria and Allowed scope against bots/story-points.md, with no access to costs, PRs or history. A spec that only pointed at a design doc was sized from that doc. Cost and working time come from the engine's ticket timeline (refine runs excluded); tokens come from each Smith run's final result event in the logs.

Limits: one model pointed, after the fact, from the final ticket text rather than the original ask, so it may have been helped by specs refined to fit the work. Bugs depend on how hard the cause is to find, which no spec shows. A split feature's own row understates its size; read the split table for those.
